What Determines the Cost of Interior Design in Nigeria? A Transparent BOQ Breakdown.

Demystifying the raw material cost drivers: marine plywood versus MDF, sintered porcelain slabs versus imported marble, and architectural LED drivers versus generic strip lights.

Chukwudi Brist Principal Architect & Founder
September 24, 2026 7 Min Read (~980 Words)

When property owners in Nigeria request an interior design quote, they are often bewildered by the vast divergence in pricing across contractors. One quote might suggest that a 5-bedroom duplex in Owerri can be fully finished for ₦15 million, while another established architectural studio quotes ₦45 million for the exact same floor plan.

Why does this discrepancy exist? In Nigeria’s construction ecosystem, the phrase "interior design" is used casually to describe everything from roadside furniture staging to rigorous interior architecture. Without understanding the fundamental material grades, hardware origins, and technical engineering specified in a Bill of Quantities (BOQ), property owners frequently compare apples to poisoned chalices.

1. The Fallacy of the "Per Square Metre" Myth

Contractors who quote a flat rate per square metre across an entire residence are inevitably either inexperienced or concealing future variation claims. A 30 sq.m dry kitchen featuring continuous waterfall quartz, integrated induction extraction, and soft-close German tandem boxes has radically different capital requirements than a 30 sq.m guest bedroom requiring minimal built-in joinery.

Cost is dictated by three pillars: structural substrate density, hardware longevity, and the precision of architectural lighting drivers.

2. Joinery Substrates: Marine Plywood vs. MDF vs. Particle Board

The single greatest invisible cost in any Nigerian interior is the wood used inside built-in wardrobes, vanity units, and kitchen carcasses:

  • Commercial Particle Board (Chipboard): The cheapest option on the market. In Nigeria’s humid rainy seasons, chipboard acts like a sponge, absorbing airborne moisture, expanding, and causing hinges to tear out within 18 months.
  • Standard MDF (Medium-Density Fibreboard): Excellent for smooth painted finishes on ceiling profiles, but vulnerable to direct water leaks behind sinks and dishwashers.
  • Calibrated Marine Plywood (WBP Grade): The BRISTS standard. Constructed with waterproof phenolic glue and cross-laminated hardwood veneers. It will survive plumbing leaks, high humidity, and termite pressure for decades without warping.
"If a kitchen quote appears suspiciously low, ask to see the carcass sample. A contractor saving ₦4,000,000 upfront on chipboard will cost you ₦12,000,000 in full kitchen replacement three years later." — Chukwudi Brist, Principal Architect

3. Surface Architecture: Sintered Slabs vs. Natural Marble vs. Local Quartz

Natural Italian marble (such as Carrara or Statuario) possesses timeless romantic appeal, but its calcite composition makes it porous and vulnerable to acidic staining from citrus, palm oil, or stew. For Nigerian kitchens and high-traffic vanity consoles, BRISTS specifies sintered porcelain slabs (such as Neolith or Dekton equivalents).

Engineered under 25,000 tons of pressure at 1200°C, sintered stone is completely non-porous, scratch-proof, and impervious to hot pots placed directly on its surface. While sintered slabs cost approximately 25% more in material purchase and require specialized diamond water-jet fabrication, their zero-maintenance lifespan protects the asset indefinitely.

Hardware Makes or Breaks Cabinetry

A wardrobe door is opened 3 to 5 times daily. Generic zinc alloy hinges begin to sag and rub within months, scraping expensive wood veneer. At BRISTS, we exclusively specify authentic Blum (Austria) or Hettich (Germany) soft-close hardware tested for 200,000 opening cycles.

4. Architectural Lighting: High-CRI Optics vs. Generic Strips

Lighting is the emotional soul of an interior, yet it is where unscrupulous contractors cut the deepest corners. Cheap LED strip lights produce uneven diode hot-spots, hum when dimmed, and emit a sickly blue-grey cast (CRI < 70) that drains warmth from skin tones and wood veneers.

Professional architectural lighting utilizes CRI 95+ chip-on-board (COB) LEDs powered by remote, flicker-free Mean Well or Osram drivers. These allow continuous dimming down to 1% without buzzing, providing circadian warmth (2700K to 3000K) that elevates a home into a luxury resort at night.

6. Summary: Protecting Your Investment

Quality interior architecture is an investment in durable daily comfort, acoustic peace of mind, and capital property appreciation. By demanding an itemized BOQ with exact brand specifications, you ensure that every naira spent delivers lasting substance rather than superficial gloss.
